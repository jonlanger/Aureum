Aureum Finance · Design System

Money, made legible.

The foundations, components, illustration and data-visualization system behind Aureum — an AI coach that helps people see what's coming and act with confidence. Built to feel friendly without losing the credibility money demands.

01 · Brand

The twin-tree mark

Two trees of different heights — one established, one growing — read as a lowercase “Aa”. It carries the brand idea: steady growth, side by side with a guide.

Clear space

Keep one mark-stem width (≈ ⅙ of mark height) clear on every side. Minimum size 20px on screen.

Voice

Warm, plain, specific. “You're $150 over on dining — want to rebalance?” not “Budget variance detected.”

Wordmark

Lora Bold, −1% tracking. Always teal mark + ink word on light; white or mint on dark.

02 · Color

Teal leads. Indigo grounds.

Teal #008080 is the brand surface — bold hero fields, the mark, the accent. Indigo #1B1B6F is the ink: headlines, primary actions, figures. Mint and sky give illustrations their air. Components reference semantic roles only, so dark mode is a token swap.

03 · Data color

A chart palette that's validated, not eyeballed

Eight categorical hues drawn from the brand, assigned in a fixed order. The order is what makes them safe for color-blind readers: every pair of neighbours stays distinct under deuteranopia, protanopia and tritanopia. Light and dark are separately tuned sets, both validated against their own surface.

Categorical · fixed order

Validation results

  • Light: worst adjacent CVD ΔE 11.8 (target ≥ 8), normal-vision ΔE 25.6 (floor 15), all in lightness band and above chroma floor
  • Dark: worst adjacent CVD ΔE 13.0, normal-vision ΔE 20.6, all ≥ 3:1 on #131C24
  • Scatter / bubble: slots 1–3 pass all-pairs in both modes. Past three series, fold into “Other” or facet.
  • Relief rule: gold (slot 5) is under 3:1 on light. It always ships with a direct label or the table view.

Sequential · teal

Magnitude: heatmaps, density. One hue, light → dark.

Diverging · teal ⇄ coral

Polarity: money in vs out, above vs below plan. Grey midpoint means “nothing.”

Status · reserved, always with icon + label

06 · Motion

Calm by default. Playful when you make progress.

UI moves with a quick, decelerating ease-out. Springy overshoot is saved for moments of delight: a goal reached, a chip appearing, an illustration. Charts draw in once when they scroll into view. Everything respects prefers-reduced-motion.

Rounded line icons

Meet Sprout, and friends

Native vector art with chunky indigo outlines, teal and mint fills, a gold accent and white sparkles. The cast now includes diverse people alongside the mascots. Every piece has a gentle idle loop, but the rules below keep it rare, spaced out and on-message.

Placement: subtle by default

Size tiers

Three sizes only. Pick by how much the moment matters.

Hero160–240px · onboarding, milestones
Spot80–140px · empty states, feature cards
Inline40–56px · nudge avatar, portrait

Do · one per screen, with air

The art sits beside the message it supports, facing the content.

Don't · crowd or stack

Three characters fighting for attention, one of them sitting on a chart.

Rules
  • One illustration per screen. A second is allowed only at inline size (≤ 56px, e.g. a nudge avatar) in a different region.
  • Keep characters at least 2× their own width apart, and at least 24px from any number, chart or table.
  • One loop in view. Extra art gets data-still. Off-screen art pauses automatically. Reduced motion stops everything.
  • Match the moment using the table above. If you can't name what the art says, cut it.
  • Rotate the cast. Vary age, skin tone, hair, family shape and ability across a flow, and portray everyone as capable.
Don't
  • Put characters inside chart cards. An empty state may replace a chart, never decorate one.
  • Deliver bad news with a character. Overdrafts and missed payments get an icon and plain words.
  • Tie a person to a money problem by appearance. Never show "older person = medical bills" or "young person = debt".
  • Recolor art with status or data colors, or crop a character at the edge of the screen.

11 · Data visualization

Charts that answer “am I okay?”

Pick the chart by the job the data does, then apply color by role. Every chart is interactive (crosshair or per-mark tooltips), keyboard-readable with arrow keys, ships a data table for screen readers, and draws itself in once on scroll. Hover, tab, or toggle Table to try it.

Area · net worth

Use for change over time when one series is the story. 2px line, 12% wash, value at the end.

Multi-line · category trend

Use to compare up to 4 series on the same scale. Legend plus end labels. One tooltip lists every series.

Projection · goal forecast with likely range

Use for the future. Solid line for actuals, dotted line for the forecast, and a widening band for uncertainty. “Today” marks where they meet. This was Lisa's top request: “tell me what's about to go wrong.”

Columns · emphasis

Use to compare periods. One color for one series. Highlight the period that matters and grey out the rest.

Diverging columns · cash flow

Use for polarity. Money in rises in teal, money out falls in coral, and the net line rides on top. One axis, one unit.

Donut · where it went

Use for part-to-whole at a glance, with 6 segments at most. The center shows the hovered value.

Stacked bar · allocation

Use for a compact part-to-whole with exact values. Segments are separated by 2px gaps, never outlines.

Gauge · financial health score

Use for one bounded score with thresholds. The fill takes the status color of its zone.

Budget meters

Show progress against a limit. The fill turns from teal to amber to red as you use more. The track is a lighter step of the same color. Status always comes with an icon and label.

Calendar heatmap · spending rhythm

Use to show patterns in daily magnitude with sequential teal: weekends, paydays, spikes. Hover any day.

12 · Chart rules

Rules every chart follows

Do
  • Choose the form first and color last. If the story is one number, use a stat tile.
  • Use bars ≤ 24px thick with 4px rounded data ends, 2px lines, and markers ≥ 8px with a 2px surface ring.
  • Keep gridlines as solid, recessive hairlines. Label selectively: the endpoint, the extreme, the one that matters.
  • Show a legend for 2+ series and direct-label 4 or fewer. Color follows the entity, never its rank.
  • Make tooltips enhance, never gate. Every value is also in the table view.
Don't
  • Use dual y-axes. Index to a common base or split into two charts.
  • Generate a 9th hue. Fold extra categories into “Other” or use small multiples.
  • Use status colors (good / warn / bad) to mean “series 4”.
  • Put a number on every point, use dashed gridlines, or outline marks to separate them.
  • Set figures in the serif. All numbers are Plus Jakarta Sans.
